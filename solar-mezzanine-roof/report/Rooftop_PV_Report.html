1. Executive Summary

This report sets out the design basis, layout, energy yield and budget cost for a grid-tied rooftop solar photovoltaic (PV) system on the mezzanine-level roof of the Marriott Hotel, as shown on architectural drawing D18102-DD-A-104. The system has no battery storage and supplies the hotel's own load behind the utility meter.

Key findings

  • Capacity. The two roof zones allocated for PV by the architect (≈1,150 m² gross, including the hatched +4.85 m slab strip) accommodate 299 modules of 620 Wp in 13 rows of 23, giving 185.4 kWp at 10° tilt with 800 mm maintenance aisles and 1.0 m edge setbacks.
  • Self-consumption. The hotel consumes approximately 1,000,000 kWh per month (average demand ≈1.37 MW). The PV peak output (≈160 kW AC) is around one-tenth of the daytime load, so 100 % of the solar energy is consumed on site. No energy is exported and no battery is required.
  • Energy and savings. Year-1 generation is about 274 MWh, or 2.3 % of the annual consumption. At an avoided tariff of USD 0.12/kWh this saves about USD 32,923 in year 1.
  • Cost and return. The budget turnkey (EPC) cost is USD 109,278 excluding taxes, including 7 % contingency. Simple payback is 3.4 years, project IRR 30 % and 25-year NPV (10 %) USD 207,408. The levelised cost of solar energy is about USD 0.053/kWh, below the grid tariff.
  • Path to 500 kWp. The architect's solar zone limits this roof to 185.4 kWp. The balance of ≈315 kWp would need other roof areas or car-park canopies (Section 8).

Recommendation

Proceed with the 185.4 kWp mezzanine roof system as Phase 1, subject to (i) a structural check of both slabs for an added dead load of about 20–25 kg/m² (including ballast) and wind uplift, and (ii) confirmation of the avoided tariff from recent utility bills. In parallel, survey other roof areas and the car park for Phase 2.

4.3 Single-line arrangement

  1. PV strings → 6 mm² double-insulated PV cable in UV-resistant trunking → inverters mounted on the roof in a shaded, ventilated location.
  2. Inverters (400 V, 3-phase) → AC combiner panel with outgoing 250 A MCCB, Type II SPD and an isolator at the roof.
  3. AC combiner panel → 4C × 120 mm² Cu XLPE/SWA (≈80 m, confirm route) → dedicated 250 A outgoing way on the main LV switchboard.
  4. Zero-export controller with CTs on the incomer limits PV output so the site never exports, satisfying utility interface requirements.
  5. Earthing: array frames bonded and connected to the building earth; integrated with the existing lightning protection system (IEC 62305).

At the equator the sun crosses close to overhead and day length is nearly constant, so monthly output is fairly even. It is lowest in the main rainy seasons (March–May and October–November). Because the hotel load exceeds the PV output in every hour, cloudy days reduce savings but never cause curtailment.

The yield figure is a desktop estimate for an equatorial Ugandan site. A PVsyst simulation using the exact site coordinates and Meteonorm/PVGIS data is recommended at detailed design.

9. Assumptions, Exclusions and Next Steps

9.1 Key assumptions

  • Roof geometry is taken from drawing D18102-DD-A-104; the hatched +4.85 m slab strip is available for PV, as confirmed by the client.
  • Both roof slabs can carry the PV dead load and wind loads without strengthening, subject to verification by the structural engineer.
  • The hotel load is about 1,000,000 kWh per month with a continuous daytime demand well above 160 kW.
  • The avoided tariff is USD 0.12/kWh escalating at 2 %/yr, to be confirmed from the latest utility bills (energy plus demand components).
  • Prices are 2026 East African market levels in USD, excluding taxes. The exchange rate is not considered.

9.2 Exclusions

  • Battery storage, diesel-generator synchronisation and export metering.
  • Roof waterproofing renewal, structural strengthening, and civil works to the LV room.
  • Utility network upgrades, permit fees and financing costs.

9.3 Recommended next steps

  1. Obtain 12 months of utility bills or interval-meter data and confirm the tariff structure.
  2. Commission a structural assessment of both slabs (dead load, ballast, wind uplift) and agree the roof fixing method with the architect.
  3. Confirm the inverter location, AC cable route and spare way on the main LV switchboard.
  4. Run a PVsyst simulation with site coordinates and the selected module and inverter datasheets.
  5. Issue the tender package (layout, single-line diagram, specification, BOQ) to at least three qualified EPC contractors.
  6. Start the Phase 2 survey of other roofs and the car park to move towards the 500 kWp target.
